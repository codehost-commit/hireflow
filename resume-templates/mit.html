Why an engineering-forward layout matters

Engineering resumes benefit from a visual language that signals precision. Sans-serif typography, tight spacing, structured section rules, and a restrained accent color read as "I care about how things are made." Compare that to a florid template with color blocks and icons, which reads to engineering recruiters as "focused on the wrong things."

The MIT template captures that engineered feel: Helvetica body text, a crimson accent rule under the header, and small caps section headings in the accent color. Bullet points use a chevron marker (▸) instead of the standard round bullet, reinforcing the technical aesthetic without hurting ATS parsing.

Like every Applio template, it's single-column, uses standard section headings, and parses cleanly on Workday, Greenhouse, Ashby, and Lever.

MIT template, FAQ

Is this an official MIT resume template?

No. It's a template inspired by the technical aesthetic associated with MIT. MIT does not publish or endorse third-party templates.

Who should use the MIT resume template?

Engineers, applied scientists, computer science students, research engineers, and PhDs applying to industry or research positions.

Is the MIT resume template ATS-friendly?

Yes. Single-column, standard headings, Helvetica typography. Parses cleanly on every major ATS.
